Shareholder Disagreements and Corporate Records

Background: A shareholder dispute may contain several issues

Founders, investors and managers may disagree about capital, leadership or transaction approvals. Treating every concern as one 'shareholder dispute' obscures different rights and records. Separate contributions, information access, board or shareholder decisions, external contracts and profit distribution to identify commercial choices and procedural questions.

Match decision-making authority to procedure

Identify who could propose, deliberate, approve and implement the matter under company type, articles, shareholder agreements and actual practice. Review notices, attendance, materials, voting and signed versions in order.

Commercial justification does not cure every procedural issue, but disagreement with a decision is not proof of illegality. Mere awareness of negotiations is not consent to specific guarantees or prices.

Read company law and internal documents together

The Company Act governs organs, resolutions and shareholder rights; articles, shareholder agreements and delegations shape individual cases. Civil-law contract and agency rules may also govern outward commitments. Compare the versions effective when decisions were made, who participated and the approved scope. Neither a job title nor one minute entry alone decides legality.

The same decision may have multiple versions

Meeting materials may differ from signed terms; an agenda may be delivered without attachments; negotiation authority may be mistaken for signing authority; related-party interests may not be disclosed. Records spread across mail, chats and cloud drives make it hard to know which version reached decision-makers. Preserve original messages, attachments and sign-off evidence rather than relying on later summaries.

Map the route before choosing a remedy

Draw a sequence from proposal and disclosure through authority, resolution, implementation and external document. Mark the unresolved points. Then compare supplemental information, fresh resolutions, negotiation and legal proceedings, considering time, cost, working relationships and the company's continued operations. Focused mapping helps separate evidence questions from personal accusations.

Do not overwrite historical records with new decisions

Preserve invitations, delivery logs, agendas, annexes, voting, delegation and signatures. A later explanation or resolution should disclose when it was created, not pretend it existed earlier.

Protect confidential investor information and observe deadlines. Where one person acts for both the company and personal interests, distinguish those roles and maintain an appropriate communication channel.

Prepare rights and facts in layers

Bring company registration, historical articles, shareholder registers, contracts, notices, minutes, attendance and votes, proposal versions, correspondence and signed agreements. Summarise key dates and the desired result: reversing a decision, curing a procedural defect, improving governance or negotiating an exit. Identify missing records without deciding the legal conclusion in advance.

Related reading: Documenting Corporate Decision-Making Authority

Example: Investment conditions change after approval

A board approves an investment sum and basic terms, then negotiations add guarantees, information rights and exit provisions. Compare original proposals, deliberations, delegation wording, amendments and the counterparty's knowledge before deciding whether further approval is needed. A change alone does not establish any person's legal liability.

Preserve rights while managing relationships

First preserve evidence and meet deadlines; second reconstruct authority, information and implementation; third select negotiation, governance reform or litigation.

Company staff, customers and financing commitments require business continuity. Preservation of rights and relationships need not be opposites: define interim roles, responsible people and review dates, and consider external commitments separately.

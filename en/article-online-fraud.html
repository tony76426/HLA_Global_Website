Online Fraud: Organizing Payments and Messages

Demands for unlocking fees, deposits, extra tax or another investment before withdrawal are warning signs. Stop paying and sharing passwords, account details or verification codes. Contact police or Taiwan's official 165 anti-fraud channel as appropriate, and notify your bank. Reporting does not guarantee a freeze or refund. Beware of follow-up scams impersonating banks or investigators.

Key Takeaways

  • Stop further loss when warning signs appear
  • Distinguish civil breach from criminal fraud
  • Keep complete conversations and identify contacts

Stop further loss when warning signs appear

Demands for unlocking fees, deposits, extra tax or another investment before withdrawal are warning signs. Stop paying and sharing passwords, account details or verification codes.

Contact police or Taiwan's official 165 anti-fraud channel as appropriate, and notify your bank. Reporting does not guarantee a freeze or refund. Beware of follow-up scams impersonating banks or investigators.

Distinguish civil breach from criminal fraud

Contractual nonperformance and criminal fraud under Criminal Code Article 339 have different elements. Late delivery, poor quality or business failure do not by themselves prove fraud, but a written contract does not erase deception concerning identity or payment.

Document pre-payment statements, reasons for relying on them, transfers and later discrepancies. Criminal and civil recovery routes require separate assessment.

Keep complete conversations and identify contacts

Preserve platform accounts, URLs, advertisements, usernames, contact information and full chats with timestamps and context, preferably as original exports. Before an account disappears, make lawful backups.

Names and identity documents supplied by a counterparty may be stolen; distinguish verified identity from clues. Give information to investigators without publicly accusing an unverified person or exposing private data.

List every payment separately

For each transfer record the date, amount, method, recipient account or platform, transaction identifier and purpose. Retain banking, e-payment, card or virtual-asset proof as appropriate.

Record refunds and small earlier returns as well as losses. If another person paid, document who and why; for crypto, retain network, addresses and hashes without assuming every address has the same owner.

Turn evidence into a report timeline

Prepare a one-page chronology of first contact, promises, payments, warning signs and current loss, then attach full evidence. Keep police intake details, contacts and later submissions by date.

Separate direct observations, online statements and inferences. Supplement new accounts or demands as evidence arises. Filing a police report and pursuing civil claims differ; deadlines do not automatically stop because one procedure is ongoing.

Check the responsible person and assets before recovery

The account holder, contracting party and actual wrongdoer may be different people. Identify the legal basis and evidence before choosing criminal-attached civil, ordinary civil or other proceedings.

Preservation measures such as provisional attachment require legal conditions and cost analysis. No process guarantees that a receiving account can be frozen or every dollar recovered; beware of unknown paid 'recovery agencies'.

Secure accounts and keep case records updated

If passwords, verification details or remote-access permissions were disclosed, check accounts and devices promptly through trusted channels, not links supplied by suspects. Keep notices of identity misuse.

Organise chats, financial transactions, contracts and official documents into four folders and update one master list. Accurate preservation and timely action matter more than naming a criminal offence perfectly.

Documents for consultation

Bring platform URLs and accounts, complete chat and advertisement files, itemised transactions, contracts and refunds, later demands, police reports, bank notifications and supplement records. Keep originals.

Frequently asked questions

Will police reporting guarantee a bank refund?

No; reporting, investigation and repayment are separate.

Does knowing the recipient account prove its owner committed fraud?

No; a holder, controller and actor may be different people, and legal responsibility needs evidence.

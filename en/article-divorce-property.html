Key Takeaways

  • Identify the applicable matrimonial property regime
  • Distinguish title, equalisation and debts
  • Trace premarital, postmarital and gratuitous assets

Identify the applicable matrimonial property regime

Whether the spouses elected separate property or another regime determines the method of settlement. If the statutory regime applies, Civil Code Article 1030-1 and related rules govern distribution of differences in remaining property.

Post-marriage acquisition does not automatically create half ownership, nor does sole registration foreclose a possible claim. Document marriage dates, property-regime agreements and changes.

Distinguish title, equalisation and debts

Ownership of a home, a claim to distribute differences in marital property and liability to a bank are connected but separate. An internal agreement assigning mortgage payments does not itself release the other spouse under the external contract.

For each asset record the registered owner, acquisition date and funding, current status and associated liabilities. Mark disputed loans, gifts and advances without double counting.

Trace premarital, postmarital and gratuitous assets

Civil Code Article 1030-1 contains exclusions, including specified inherited or gratuitously acquired property and consolation payments. An exclusion requires evidence of the source; 'my parents gave it to me' may not be sufficient.

Keep succession or gift documents, purchase records and bank transfers. Trace proceeds when an asset is sold, reinvested or used to pay a loan; mixed funds require particular care.

Check valuation dates and evidence

The relevant termination or calculation date and proper value differ according to the governing rule and case. Homes, unlisted shares and fluctuating investments may need appraisals or comparable transactions.

Check liabilities as of the same relevant date, rather than combining today's market value with an old debt figure. State whether each value is formally appraised, sourced from a transaction or merely provisional; consider the cost of litigation appraisal.

Recognise domestic labour and fairness considerations

The statutory framework concerns equalisation and possible fair adjustments, not salaries alone. Housework, childcare and assistance with the family's economic life can be relevant contributions even where one spouse earned less.

Describe actual care schedules, employment changes and household responsibilities rather than pricing every chore separately. Any adjustment or exemption requires a legal, fact-specific assessment.

Investigate asset transfers without assumptions

Large withdrawals, share transfers or property disposals during divorce discussions should be recorded with dates, recipients and available explanations. Not every transfer is fraudulent dissipation, and one should not seize another person's property in retaliation.

Additional calculation, recovery and protective measures involve different legal issues. Obtain account and communications evidence lawfully and preserve the originals.

Draft precise settlement and performance terms

A settlement should specify which assets and claims are covered, how compensation is calculated, dates of payment, transfers, taxes, loans and document delivery. Lump sums, instalments and continued co-ownership carry different risks.

Civil Code Article 1030-1 contains limitation rules that depend on awareness and termination dates; talks do not automatically suspend them. Understand any sweeping waiver before signing.

Documents for consultation

Bring property-regime and marriage-date records, acquisition contracts, proof of funds, bank and investment statements, mortgages, inheritance and gift evidence, and records of childcare and domestic work. Identify gaps and retain originals.

Frequently asked questions

Is a house bought after marriage always owned half each?

No: legal title and remaining-property equalisation differ.

Must gifts from parents be distributed?

Some gratuitously acquired assets are excluded under the law, but the gift, recipient and later movement of funds must be proved.

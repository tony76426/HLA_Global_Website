The purpose of parent-child contact

Visitation and contact arrangements should put the child's best interests first, allowing appropriate relationships with a parent who does not live with the child. Article 1055 of Taiwan's Civil Code addresses determining and changing contact methods and periods.

A child's age, health, prior relationship and travel distance matter; a schedule suitable for one family is not automatically suitable for another. Safety concerns or little prior contact may require gradual, professionally supported arrangements.

Specify times and handover arrangements

An agreement should state the start and end times, meeting point, transport, person responsible for pickup and what happens if someone is late. Vague phrases such as free visitation on holidays create recurring disputes.

Select handover places based on familiarity, traffic and conflict risks. A neutral location or third-party support may help, but a child should not be left to coordinate adults or resolve disputes.

Coordinate school holidays, festivals and special days

Weekends, summer and winter holidays, birthdays and major festivals may overlap. Agree on priority rules, notice periods and make-up contact while respecting school, rest and activities.

When parents live in different cities, distinguish short visits from longer holiday stays and allocate travel duties and costs. Cross-border visits and travel abroad also require attention to documents, consent and safety.

Calls and video contact need age-appropriate boundaries

Phone and video contact can maintain relationships but must fit the child's routine and wishes. Long daily calls or constant reporting may create pressure; set sensible windows and simple alternatives when school, illness or sleep intervenes.

Adults should exchange education, medical and litigation information directly rather than using the child to investigate another parent's personal life. Respect privacy and avoid compulsory recording or interrogation.

Keep neutral records of cancelled or delayed contact

Illness, weather or school events may justify changes. Notify the other parent with a concrete reason and proposed alternative, retaining relevant evidence.

One legitimate cancellation differs from systematic obstruction. Track the scheduled time, notification, actual contact and next steps objectively; a complete pattern helps distinguish unclear terms, practical difficulty and safety concerns.

Understand why a child resists contact

When a child refuses contact, examine age, feelings, earlier experiences and possible harm rather than resorting to force or blame. Nor should one refusal automatically end all future contact without appropriate assessment.

Gradual reconnection, amended methods or professional support may be needed. Parents should neither coach a child toward a position nor treat a single statement as the only evidence; credible abuse risks require protective procedures.

When negotiation fails, follow the legal documents

Check any existing judgment or mediation terms and their enforceable scope. Taiwan's Family Proceedings Act contains special arrangements for the enforcement of child handover and contact, requiring attention to the child's welfare rather than treating the matter like seizure of property.

A lawyer can assess enforcement, modification or interim relief. Unpaid child support and contact should be addressed separately, not used as bargaining leverage against the child.

Documents to prepare before consultation

Bring the original agreement, mediation or judgment; the child's school and medical schedule; each parent's residence and transport circumstances; records of pickup, cancellations and notices; and specific proposed changes. List missing evidence and preserve original files.

Frequently asked questions about contact

May I cancel next time if the other parent is late?

Do not use cancellation as unilateral punishment; document the problem and consider revision or court relief if it repeatedly harms the child.

Does a child's refusal automatically end contact?

No. Understand the reasons and any safety risk, seek age-appropriate support and review the child's best interests and existing orders before changing or restricting contact.

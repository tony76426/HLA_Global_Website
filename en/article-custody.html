Parental authority, residence and maintenance differ

Parental authority concerns the exercise of rights and duties toward a minor. Whether one parent or both hold that authority, where the child primarily lives and how financial support is shared require separate decisions.

Joint authority does not automatically mean equal nights; sole authority does not extinguish the other parent's support duty. Civil Code Articles 1055 and 1055-1 frame agreements and judicial determinations in the child's interests.

Document care as a verifiable schedule

Record who normally takes the child to school, appointments and activities, speaks with teachers, cooks and handles overnight care. 'I have always done everything' is less useful than weekday, holiday and emergency examples.

Describe work, home and family support. School and medical records may assist, but preserve context and privacy; do not automatically blame a parent for a child's performance or mood.

Respect the child's voice without pressure

The child's wishes and development are among relevant factors, but age, understanding and circumstances of expression matter. A single recording or preference is not a complete answer; do not repeatedly demand that a child choose between parents.

Courts may consider social-worker reports and family investigations. Parents should provide genuine life records and feasible plans, not coach interview answers. Professional support should serve the child's wellbeing.

Income is not the sole measure

Income, health, living conditions and ability to care all matter, but the higher earner does not automatically receive parental authority. Everyday care, stable relationships and reliable support networks are also relevant.

Explain how the child's needs can be met with available resources. Identify who can actually help with transport and emergencies, not just a list of relatives. Revisit a plan when work or housing changes.

Cooperation and safety need different treatment

Respecting the child's relationship with the other parent, sharing information and managing handovers matters. Yet violence, threats or inappropriate contact call for protection, not an assumption that the protective parent is uncooperative.

Document incidents and safeguards for legal review. Unjustified obstruction of contact may also affect assessment. Neither parent should disparage the other to the child or use the child to relay litigation messages.

Build contingency arrangements into the plan

Specify weekday residence, holidays, contact, medical and education decisions, and substitute carers when needed. Long travel distances and pickup burdens should be realistic.

Family mediation or court proceedings may help resolve disputes; urgent needs can justify asking about provisional measures. Temporary arrangements and final decisions serve different functions. Compare all proposals against the child's current needs.

Change an unsuitable arrangement through proper process

Taiwanese law provides mechanisms to revise arrangements harmful to a child or when parental duties are not fulfilled. Set out what has changed since the earlier agreement, present risks and a feasible replacement; dissatisfaction with an ex-partner alone is insufficient.

Do not unilaterally remove a child, conceal the address or abruptly change schools to create a fait accompli. Deal with immediate danger through appropriate protective procedures.

Documents for consultation

Prepare daily and holiday care schedules, education and medical records, both parents' housing and work hours, available support, existing agreements, orders or protection measures, and a realistic primary and backup plan. Identify missing records and preserve originals.

Frequently asked questions

Does higher income decide parental authority?

No: care, relationships, the child's wishes and other factors also matter.

Does joint authority mean an equal split of nights?

No: residence, transport and decision-making must be designed around the child's needs.

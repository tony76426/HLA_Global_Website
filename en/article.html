Background: Signing is only one step in a transaction

A business contract usually moves from sales quotations to finance review, technical specifications and an authorised signature. If departments retain different versions, disputes may follow over delivery scope, acceptance or notice deadlines despite the signing. The process of forming a decision and performing the agreement should be reconstructed together, not judged solely from the signature page.

Related reading: Organizing Transaction Documents and Milestones

Key issues: Authority, agreement and performance

First identify who can approve, represent and instruct. Next establish the actual agreement across contracts, annexes, quotations, email and later changes. Finally map evidence of performance: delivery records, acceptance, payments and objections. These questions should connect in one dated sequence; reviewing authority, consensus and actual conduct in isolation makes practical implementation difficult.

Apply the current law and the complete agreement

Business transactions involve Taiwan Civil Code rules on contracts and debt performance, plus company law, articles of association and actual delegations. A clause's effect depends on the transaction purpose, correspondence, performance and subsequent conduct, not one sentence in isolation. Special legislation or industry permits may apply. Review the law effective at the relevant time, the complete documents and actual facts, rather than treating templates as universal answers.

Related reading: Documenting Corporate Decision-Making Authority

Common gaps: Versions and milestones do not align

A schedule might not be signed with the main agreement, specifications might be changed by messaging without a formal version, payment conditions might conflict with acceptance, or sales might promise deadlines before internal approval. Delay disputes then turn on start dates, opportunities to cure and damage calculations. Such problems often stem from inconsistent documents, responsibilities and timelines; identifying them early clarifies what needs negotiation.

Make a practical legal decision register

Record the business goal, decision-maker, key duties, deliverables, due dates, accountable owner and evidence for each step. Before signing, check annex versions, notices, cure periods, termination handover and dispute clauses. After signing, track payments, delivery and acceptance. If gaps emerge, separate verified facts, each side's position and acceptable commercial outcomes before selecting notices, negotiation or proceedings. Counsel supports judgment; the company makes its own business choices.

Preserve records that another team can understand

Keep original timestamps, recipients, version numbers and attachments for important discussions. Summaries help but do not replace originals. Confirm verbal changes promptly, recording scope and responsibility. Control access to personal data and trade secrets. Under urgent deadlines preserve evidence and verify notification methods; do not delete unfavourable records merely to speed negotiation. Case-specific rights and remedies require professional review.

Documents for an initial legal review

Bring the main agreement and all attachments, recent and historical versions, authority and resolution records, prices and specifications, payment and delivery evidence, acceptance documents and communications. Summarise event dates, the next deadline and whether the company hopes to preserve or revise the relationship. Mark missing information rather than guessing conclusions; a complete working set distinguishes legal risks from commercial options.

Example: Specifications change shortly before delivery

A customer requests new requirements; sales agrees orally but no one confirms the price or timetable, engineers follow the old schedule, and the client plans acceptance under the new version. Collect meeting notes, messages and each specification version. Determine who proposed and could approve the change, and whether mutual agreement arose under the contract's change clause. Then consider a written supplement, revised milestone or dispute process without presuming either party's liability.

Prioritise preservation, then compare options

Separate action into what must be preserved today and deadlines approaching, facts and authority to verify this week, and longer-term choices about cure, amendment, termination or proceedings. Assign owners and document sources for each step.

Compare legal feasibility with supply, customer relationships, cash flow and implementability. Begin legal review early by naming decision-makers, payment triggers and acceptance evidence; record open questions and review dates to prevent another cycle of uncertainty.

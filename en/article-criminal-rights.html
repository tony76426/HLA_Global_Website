Before Police Questioning: Rights and Evidence

Verify the notice and your procedural status

Keep written notices, calls and contact details; verify the authority, officer, appearance time and required documents through official channels if needed. Beware of requests from strangers for transfers or account information.

A suspect, defendant, witness and complainant may have different rights and obligations. Show the actual notice and known events to counsel instead of guessing a convenient story.

Related reading: After Receiving an Investigation Notice

Understand silence, counsel and favourable evidence

Taiwan Code of Criminal Procedure Article 95 requires notice of relevant rights before questioning a defendant, including the allegation, silence, legal representation and requesting investigation of favourable evidence; related rules apply to police interviews of suspects.

These rights support informed choices, not fabrication. Decide whether and how to answer in light of the case, and ask about legal aid when eligible.

Use a timeline, not rehearsed answers

List dates, places, people and source documents. Separate what you personally observed from what others told you and from later inferences; mark uncertain amounts and times for verification.

Do not agree on stories with others, delete conversations or invent documents. Give counsel unfavourable evidence as well as favourable material, so the assessment rests on the complete record.

Preserve original and lawfully obtained evidence

Save original conversations, payments, locations, account records and videos with their sources and timestamps. Screenshots help reading but may omit decisive context.

Identify which fact each piece can establish and ask how to submit it while retaining copies. Do not intrude into others' accounts, take possessions without authority or falsify records. Protect privacy and confidential information when backing up evidence.

Clarify each question before answering

If a question is ambiguous, contains multiple assumptions or concerns something you cannot remember, request clarification and distinguish confirmed knowledge from uncertainty. Explain any need for interpretation, health assistance or other reasonable support.

If coercion or improper treatment occurs, preserve details and seek appropriate help. Raise concerns through proper channels rather than obstructing lawful procedure.

Read the record carefully before signing

An interview record should reflect your actual statement. Check dates, sums, roles, admissions and whether estimates were wrongly recorded as facts.

Request corrections or a notation of objections, then read the amended version. Ask for reading assistance or interpretation if necessary. Never sign on the assumption that the form is a mere formality, and do not deny truthful statements through fabricated explanations.

Continue tracking the process afterwards

A police interview neither ends a case nor necessarily means prosecution. Further investigation, prosecutor interviews and notices may follow, each with deadlines.

Record attendance, materials supplied and outstanding questions, and file documents by agency and date. An initial legal consultation works best with the actual notice, a complete timeline and original records, allowing a fact-based strategy.

Documents for consultation

Bring notices and agency contacts, a full event timeline, original chats and transactions, signed or submitted documents, and questions about procedural status. List missing materials and keep originals intact.

Frequently asked questions

Does a police summons automatically mean I am a defendant?

No: verify whether you are a witness, complainant or suspect.

Should I guess when memory is unclear?

No: explain what you cannot remember and what documents might verify it; request clarification and an accurate record.

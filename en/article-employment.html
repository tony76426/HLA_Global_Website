Background: Documents and actual work can diverge

Recruitment advertisements, interview promises, employment contracts, work rules and managers' instructions may conflict about pay, hours, duties or location. Old policies may lag behind reorganised teams. When appraisals, transfers or termination are disputed, the parties may point to different documents. Reconstruct the employment timeline, recording each decision's date, reason, notice and actual effect.

Check terms, process and records together

Verify the original job conditions, how later changes were agreed, and the objective basis for assessments or transfers. Were standards explained in advance, feedback specific and the employee given a chance to respond? Termination also raises separate questions of grounds, procedure, notice periods and payments. Employers need consistent policies; workers need records showing what they actually did.

Look beyond the contract's label

Taiwan's Labor Standards Act regulates employment conditions, wages, hours and termination; dispute resolution, equal employment, safety and other laws may also apply. The actual working relationship and current law matter more than a document's title. Compare internal rules, individual agreements and practices. Whether a particular transfer or dismissal is lawful requires full case-specific review.

Common disputes: Missing performance context

Performance standards sometimes appear only after a dispute; verbal targets were never recorded; a nominal reassignment changes commuting, pay or family life; or parties disagree whether departure was voluntary, agreed or imposed. A final letter alone rarely tells the whole story. Preserve earlier communications, wages, duties, feedback and dates; do not invent reasons retrospectively.

Counsel's approach: Link the employment milestones

Create a concise matrix for recruitment terms, contracts, onboarding, performance targets, feedback, changes and separation, with responsible staff and records for each. Put verifiable facts on a timeline, then separate legal minimums, contractual promises and management choices. Major decisions should have understandable reasons and next steps. Systemic gaps should be corrected alongside any individual dispute.

Keep accurate, necessary and protected records

Appraisals should describe observable performance and feedback rather than vague labels. Collect employee information only as needed, with appropriate permissions and safeguards; do not broadcast sensitive files in group chats. Preserve original messages and full context. Check legal deadlines, payments and sector-specific requirements early. Neither employers nor employees should delete unfavourable material.

Prepare an employment timeline for consultation

Bring job advertisements, employment contracts, rules, pay and attendance records, assessment standards, feedback, transfer notices, responses, separation papers and important communications. Record hiring, role changes, disputes and recent decisions with dates and any urgent deadline. If cooperation might continue, explain desired improvements and communication measures as well as legal claims.

Example: Reassignment to a more distant workplace

A department merger may move an employee farther away while nominal tasks and salary remain unchanged, increasing commuting and care burdens. Compare the contract, original conditions, business reasons, alternatives, notice and discussions under current law. The issue cannot be settled merely by asserting a management prerogative or unwillingness to cooperate; real effects and procedure matter.

Priorities: Verify facts before major action

Before evaluation-driven termination, review objectives, performance records, feedback, improvement opportunities, applicable law and rules. If the aim is improvement, explain expectations, time and support clearly.

In a dispute, preserve the original timeline and distinguish factual, legal and negotiable questions. Do not use backdated notes or a generic template as a substitute for evidence; set regular review and retention rules.

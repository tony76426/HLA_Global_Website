Background: Many files but no agreed master version

A transaction may include a main contract, schedules, purchase orders, specifications, quotations, change orders, minutes and email. These documents differ in timing, parties and legal effect. Without a document register, operations may use an obsolete specification while finance follows a different payment schedule. The first task in a delivery dispute is often to establish exactly which version became binding, and when.

Key issues: When each obligation begins and ends

Separate duties to deliver, pay, cooperate and inspect. Specify the event starting each deadline, who can certify completion, notice methods and remedies after failed acceptance. Determine whether email adjustments complied with the contractual change procedure. Linking observable actions to original evidence reveals whether a duty is truly incomplete, disputed or changed.

Related reading: Preserving Evidence and Timelines in Disputes

Assess clauses in their commercial context

Taiwan's Civil Code provides general rules on contracts, performance and breach, but concrete obligations depend on the agreement and transaction. Industry standards, permits and special rules may apply. Alongside notice and acceptance clauses, review how parties actually operated. Cross-border deals require particular care over language versions, governing law and dispute resolution.

Related reading: Jurisdiction and Governing Law in Cross-Border Contracts

Common disputes: Implied acceptance and undocumented changes

A buyer may use delivered work without formal acceptance, request revisions that the supplier views as extra work, or rely on a different payment annex. Categorise records into agreed terms, proposals awaiting approval and actual practices. Preserve the full conversation, not one cropped screenshot; an internal expectation does not prove the counterparty consented.

Use two parallel timelines

On one timeline record contract milestones for payment, delivery, acceptance and notices; on another record actual conduct and evidence. Compare them to identify unmet prerequisites, unclear standards, missing notices or unapproved changes. Then distinguish urgent remedies from negotiable commercial adjustments. If the relationship can continue, better version control may be more effective than confrontation; formal claims still require deadline checks.

Important notices must be provable

Follow the contract's required notice method, address and recipient; retain both content and proof of delivery. Workplace messages may not substitute for formal service. Record the proposer, scope, price effect, effective date and milestone impact of every variation. Preserve original digital files and earlier versions rather than overwriting them during a dispute; specific rights depend on the contract and current law.

Consultation documents and business goals

Collect contracts and annexes, file versions, orders, payments, delivery evidence, inspection records, change discussions, notices and responsible personnel. Identify what is completed, ongoing and contested, and whether the goal is continued cooperation, amended terms or exit. Mark missing documents and likely holders so legal review can focus on provable risks.

Example: New requirements emerge after delivery

A supplier delivers a system under the contract and the customer begins using it but requests changes without signing an acceptance record. Compare original tests and criteria, objections, revision lists and payment terms. Identify requests outside the agreed scope separately. That distinction helps negotiate correction work, new work and the corresponding milestones rather than treating use alone as decisive.

Create a shared status register

List each deliverable, supporting evidence, unresolved issue, respondent and reply date in a common register. Explicitly distinguish one-sided assertions from agreed facts; silence should not automatically be recorded as consent.

Follow contractual methods for formal legal notices. If the relationship ends, inventory deliverables, data return and ongoing licences. Standard templates help only when populated with the specific transaction's acceptance and cure terms.
